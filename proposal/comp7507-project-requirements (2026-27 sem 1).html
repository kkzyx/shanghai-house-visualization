Introduction

In this project, you will apply visualization and visual analytics techniques on some datasets of your own choice. Specifically, you will identify some datasets in a problem domain or topic (e.g., NBA games) of your interests and work out visualizations that help understand the data/problem. You are also expected to perform visual analysis on the data, e.g., to ask questions about your visualizations, develop possible hypotheses and further verify the hypotheses.

It is expected that each of you will spend approximately a total of 60 hours on the project.

Important note: We will take plagiarism seriously, so make sure that all submissions for your project are your own work or you will risk losing marks in full for your project.

Please also check the late submission and plagiarism policies from the course information on Moodle.

Grouping

You will work in a group of 5 members for the project. Please make sure that you have indicated your grouping on Moodle, as some submissions would be done on a group basis on Moodle. Grouping cannot be changed after the submission of the proposal.

Assessment

The assessment will be based mainly on the followings:

  • how well you can apply proper visualization design rationales and techniques 
  • how well you can perform visual analysis (e.g., hypothesis generation, verification, knowledge discovery)

Weighting (course project total: 45%)

weighting assessed by
Proposal 5% group*
Demo Presentation 10% group*
Final Report (Group & Individual) + Work Product 20% individual & group*
Critique 5% individual
In-class Discussion Participation 5% individual

Note *: Up to 15% of the marks will be moderated by intragroup peer evaluation (i.e., to evaluate contributions from your group members).

General Rubrics (for final work)

Proposal

The purpose of the proposal is to help you define the project scope and consolidate your ideas. Try your best to explore and investigate the feasibility of your ideas & plans when developing your proposal. You should better identify viable data sources so that it does not take you too much time on tedious data cleaning or processing.

What to submit (for each group)

  • Complete the proposal form (provided on Moodle) and submit in PDF format. The proposal should contain:

    1. Project title - give a suitable title for your project
    2. Group number, names of students in your group, with student IDs
    3. Project objectives (about 50 words on what problem to solve or analyze with visualizations)
    4. A brief description of the datasets that you are interested in. Specify the source of the datasets. It is highly recommended to use data from multiple data sources instead of one only.
    5. Methodology. The visualization tools or approaches that you plan to use in visualizing and analyzing the datasets, and some justifications supporting your choices.
    6. A list of tasks, with a reasonable timeline, to be accomplished and the division of labour among the group members

Due credits should be given wherever appropriate to any adopted/adapted materials.

You will receive feedback on your proposal from the teaching team within 1 week after submission, but you should continue to work on the project after proposal submission without idling.

In-Class Discussions

Each group will meet with the teaching team about two times during in-class discussion sessions. The purpose of the discussion sessions is for the teaching team to understand your progress and any difficulties encountered. Each session will take about 10 mins.

What to prepare:

  • A 1-2 presentaton slides to report on your progress, including
    • what has been done
    • any difficulties encountered
    • what to do next

You will need to submit the slides after the meeting for record.

Demo Presentation

Each group will give a live demonstration of your work. You should highlight the major visualizations and visual analyses that you have accomplished.

Note that this is not a formal presentation. Instead, a walkthrough with your interactive visualizations is expected. You may not have time go through all the work that you have done, so wisely choose one or two main visualizations that best demonstrate your lines of thought during the visual analyses.

Final Work & Indivdual/Group Report

Two submissions are required:

  1. (Group submission) Final work product (e.g., Tableau dashboards) in a single zipped file

    • An index.html file that contains links for access to all the submitted files including the group & individual reports (e.g., a readme document containing instructions to try out and access your work, links to online Tableau dashboards you’ve created). Note: The index.thml file serves as a starting page to access your results and you do not need to create and design a web page for it.
    • Other results in any format that are deemed appropriate. We are flexible and so be creative in showing your work. Video clips and online demos are some of the options.

  2. (Group submission) A report in ONE SINGLE PDF:

    • Part I: Overall Work (max 20 pages, not including title page and references), which should include, but not limited to
      • Project title & objectives
      • Group number, name and student IDs
      • Highlights of your work in THREE bullet points
      • Visualizations that you have created
      • Effectiveness of the visualizations (e.g., how you use them to develop hypotheses and understand/analyse data/problems)
      • Different methods that you have tried and justification of your choices
      • Difficulties that you have encountered, if any
      • Anything you wanted to do but haven’t? Why?
      • Any limitations to your visualizations and/or the tools that you used?
      • References
    • Part II: Individual Contributions (max 5 pages FOR EACH member), which should include the followings
      • Name of the group member
      • Details of tasks completed
      • Results or findings of the tasks (include images or reference to Part I wherever appropriate)

Critique

To submit individually

The critique serve as an exercise for you to be able to appreciate and criticize others' work (to help others' improve), which should be done in an academic exchange manner.

We don't intend to create too much workload for you here, so we try to simplify the process as much as possible. You are expected to spend about an hour for the critique.

  • You will be assigned to review the demo presentation and the final work submitted by one or two other groups.
  • You will need to complete a review form for each of the groups and submit the forms to Moodle.
  • Note that this is a blind review, meaning that your identity would be hidden from the groups that you would review.

Detailed instructions will be given to you on Moodle announcements when the critique starts.
